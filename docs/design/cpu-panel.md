# CPU panel design

Status: approved to build, 2026-11-01, after a product design review, a systems review, experiment 05 and
a third review of the rewrite. Evidence is
[`measurable.md`](../measurable.md), sections "CPU counters checked" (experiment 04) and "CPU contention
checked" (experiment 05). This file cites rows by claim, and each number it repeats is one a decision
depends on. Experiment 05 is one trial per case.

## 1. The user and the job

The user is a developer or systems engineer on one Windows 11 machine who runs real work in WSL2. They
open this panel because something feels slow, or to watch a long run.

The reason this panel exists: **when Windows and WSL both want the CPU, WSL cannot see it.** In experiment
05 WSL lost 71.143% of its work while the guest read 99.997% busy, `steal` stayed 0 and PSI cpu did not
track it. Any tool inside the guest, btop and htop included, sees a healthy busy machine. Only a view
of both sides can show the loss.

In the order the user asks, each answered in words at a fixed position, from the first sample:

1. **Is the machine short of CPU, and which side is paying?**
2. **Who is it?** Which process, on which side.
3. **What is the VM costing Windows?**
4. **One thread or all of them?**
5. **When did it start?** This one needs the tool to have been running: history is memory-only and
   starts at launch.

**Not the goal:** replacing htop as a process manager. Rule 1 (read-only) stays. No kill, renice, affinity,
tree view, mouse or per-thread list.

## 2. Concept

**The top rows are the answer. The timeline below them is the evidence for it.**

- Number-first heads at fixed positions, read in seconds. Instant figures are valid from the first sample;
  `lost` and `ready` need 5 samples and say so.
- **Windows left, WSL right, equal plot widths, never a shared graph.**
- Within a column every row shares one time axis, right edge is now, so cause and effect line up top to
  bottom. No number sits inside a plot row, because it would shorten that row's time.
- Coarse to fine: total, lanes, each thread, processes.

### Allocation

Rows go in this order. At 34 it gives busy graph 3, RAM 2, lanes 2, lists 6; at 40 busy graph 9.

1. Title, rule, 4 head rows, footer, lists with 3 data rows and 2 heading rows: 12.
2. Heat block: caption, 10 rows, axis: 12 at 20 threads or more, since rows are capped by room.
3. Busy graph, 3 rows.
4. Lanes, 2 rows.
5. RAM: a 2-row plot, or at exactly 30 rows a one-row lane.
6. Lists up to 6 data rows.
7. Busy graph up to 12 rows.
8. The rest to the lists.

| window | layout |
|---|---|
| Width 100 or more, height at least 17 plus heat rows: 27 at 20 threads or more | This layout; lanes and RAM plot drop first, below 31 |
| Smaller | Today's checked panel, unchanged, on `plot_columns`, and `t` does nothing there |

One new layout, no third. Windows Terminal's default size is from memory, 120 by 30, not verified; it gets

Heads keep their columns at worst-case values. Drop order, left head row 3: the `ready` figure. Right

### The inference

`lost` is the one new inference, and the only cross-side arithmetic in the panel.

- **Where:** a stateful `lost_tracker` in `src/analysis/`, beside `gpu_split`, writing `snapshot.lost`, so
  `--record` and the screen read the same value. `wsl_view` moves to `src/core/` so analysis can read the
  guest line; `history::push` only reads the result. It is computed after `wsl_json` is set.
- **Formula:** the gap between what the guest counted and what Windows charged the VM: guest busy% times
  guest threads, less `vm.vmmem_cores`, each a 5-sample mean, clamped at 0. A subtraction across the two
  sides, on purpose, and recorded as a decision: it does not add overlapping resources, and it is
  labelled `inferred`. Guest busy is what the guest wanted, not what it got, which is why the gap means
  something. Case means: 5.521, 10.372, 14.244 cores against 5.359, 9.787, 14.288 lost.
- **Valid only inside the evidence:** all 5 guest samples at 99% or more, because every contended case had
  WSL at 20 threads and a partly busy guest is `Not checked`, and this also stops the false fires when a
  guest load ends (replay: 3.22 and 3.65 vCPUs short on a quiet Windows under a looser gate); newest Windows
  busy at 95% or more, for the case replay does not reach; a guest line under `kStaleS` old with at most one
  consecutive repeat; 5 finite samples since the last reset. Intervals up to 2500 ms are kept, wider than
  `kGapMs`, because the loop itself runs late under contention. Otherwise `n/a`, drawn as a gap, never 0.
- **Outside the evidence:** `.wslconfig` vCPU limits, and memory, disk or GPU load. Named in the README, and
  the reason a gated `n/a` is common. A real game beside a build may read `n/a`, which is honest.
- **Floor:** under 1 vCPU it prints `<1` and draws quiet. `wsl-20` alone read 0.354 to 0.767 with nothing
  competing. It is a judgement, not a clean noise floor: the tail below passes it with nothing competing.
- **Lag:** a 5-sample mean, so the figure lags. After a Windows load stops under a full guest it fades over
  about 7 samples (`lost_check` pins them). Stated in the README.
- **Tone:** `watch` from 1 vCPU in the head, amber in the lane, because the row the panel exists for must
  not look calmer than a healthy busy machine.
- **Always labelled `inferred`** in the head and in JSON; in the lane the gutter's `│` becomes `≈`.

### Refused, and why

| asked of a btop replacement | decision |
|---|---|
| CPU temperature | "`Thermal Zone` temperature moves under sustained load": False here, and no other source was readable non-elevated. README says so |
| Clock speed | `Actual Frequency` read sane at all-core load, but it is derived, and `% of Maximum Frequency` disagrees (Unresolved). Costs a PDH query. Deferred, not refused |
| `steal` | "`steal` shows lost CPU": False here. Shown only above 0, as today, and never read as "no contention" |
| `% Processor Utility` | 131.995 against busy 100.000 at all-core. A different question; not shown |
| `Processor Queue Length` | Cannot tell which side is short |
| `Hv LP % Guest Run Time` | Is Root VP plus `Hv VP`: Windows runs as a guest too |
| Last CPU per process | "A process's `/proc/pid/stat` `processor` is where it works": False here |
| Command line | Protocol choice (`PROTOCOL.md`): arguments can hold secrets and `--record` writes them to disk. Stays out |
| `[s]` sort by memory | Serves no job above, and alone would need a wider collector line. Cut |
| Per-thread DPC and interrupt | No place in the layout, meaning `Not checked`, largest ever seen 6.250. Cut |

### Traps the screen must not fall into

- **A vCPU is not a Windows core.** "Guest `cpuN` lines up with a Windows LP while busy": No, 0 of 20. The
  caption states it above the block, because a label under a picture does not undo the picture.
- **WSL's P or E is unknown.** The guest's 10 cores of 2 threads are not the real layout. The `v` rows have
  no P or E, and equal VP time is not equal work (False here).
- **Windows kernel holds the VM,** so the split separates it out, or every WSL load would read as a
  Windows kernel problem.
- **Busy is not Task Manager's figure when `% Processor Utility` differs.** Whether Task Manager uses it was
  not checked. The README says the numbers can differ, and no more.

## 5. Visual language

The tool assumes a dark terminal, as its text colours already do: `text` is 1.67:1 on white. Light themes
stay out of scope, stated in the README, not half-fixed here.

- **Heat cell:** `▀`, foreground first thread, background second, `▄` when only the second is known.
  Teach it as 20 lines of pixels, not as pairs: only P rows are real siblings. A row holds thread pairs
  in layout order: siblings for a P-core, two neighbours for E, two in number order for `v`, one thread and
  a blank half when the count is odd. On a machine with more than 40 threads a row holds more, shows the
  highest, and the caption says `highest of each row`. The block never exceeds 20 rows.
- **Ramp**, 256-colour, lightness rising throughout:

  | step | busy | colour index | L* about |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | blank | no measurement | terminal background | |
  | quiet | under 5% | 236 | 20 |
  | low | 5 to under 30 | 60 | 38 |
  | mid | 30 to under 60 | 67 | 54 |
  | high | 60 to under `kHotPct` | 110 | 70 |
  | hot | `kHotPct` and over | 179, amber | 74 |

  The quiet boundary sits above the 1.5625-point tick, so idle threads do not twinkle. Amber at the top
  matches `hot()` everywhere else and stays on the axis colour-blind readers keep. `act` red keeps its
  rarer meaning. The legend sits in the caption, in full cells: `idle ░▒▓█ ≥90%`, five swatches in reality.
- **Colour is never the only channel:** counts and numbers in the heads say it in words.
- **Lanes** are one heat row. Left: `kern` (kernel less the VM, percent of the machine), `VM` (percent of the
  machine). Right: `iowt`, `lost` (percent of guest threads, amber from 1 vCPU). The RAM lane at 30 rows is
  the same cell. A lane replaces the first draft's one-row braille plots, which could not move below 37.5%.
- **RAM plots** are 2 rows of steel braille, 0-100, 12.5 points a dot, gutter `RAM`, same axis.
- **Busy graphs** keep braille, steel and the fixed 0-100 scale (ui rule 8). Not stacked.
- **Time buckets** are fixed: `floor(t / bucket)`, so a sample never changes cell and the block moves one
  cell at a time. Both plots use the smaller width. An empty bucket between samples at most `kGapMs` apart
  takes the previous sample, or wide terminals would stripe. Means skip NaN. The busy and RAM plots take the
  peak of a bucket, so no spike vanishes. Heat and lanes take the mean, because a one-second burst must not
  paint 20 seconds hot: on experiment 04's data peak puts many cells at 50 or more and mean puts none. The
  axis says `each cell 1.4 s`, and past 60 s `peak over mean · 20 s`.
- **Guest samples:** a repeated guest line carries the previous value for one sample. Under contention
  experiment 05 had 13 repeats in 58 records and never two in a row, because arrival slips while the guest
  keeps measuring; a gap would stripe the whole column. Two repeats in a row are a gap. This amends ui
  rule 8 and the decision that a repeated line is a gap, for this layout only.
